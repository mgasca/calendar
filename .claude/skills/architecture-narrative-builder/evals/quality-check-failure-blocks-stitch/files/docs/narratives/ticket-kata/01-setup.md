## Act 1: Setup

### The problem

A regional venue sells tickets for 40 concerts a year by phone and at the box office. Popular concerts sell out within minutes of opening, and staff take orders on paper, so seats are sometimes sold twice.

### Why now

The venue's paper process oversold 120 seats last season and refunds cost more than the season's marketing budget. If nothing changes, the venue loses the two touring promoters who supply its biggest concerts.

### Stakeholders

| Role | Needs | Decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venue manager | No oversold seats, fewer refunds | Budget and launch date |
| Box-office staff | One view of what is sold | How walk-in sales are taken |
| Fans | A fair chance at popular seats | Whether to buy |

### Users and scale

About 2,000 fans try to buy within the first ten minutes of a popular on-sale; 8 box-office staff sell in person at the same time.

### Scope

In scope: online and box-office sales, seat holds, payment. Out of scope: resale, season passes.

### Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| On-sale | The moment tickets for a concert become available. |
| Hold | A seat reserved for one buyer for up to 8 minutes while they pay. |
| Oversell | Selling the same seat to two buyers. |
| Buyer | Anyone buying a ticket: a fan online, or box-office staff selling to a fan in person. |
| Till | A box-office sales terminal; it can sell offline for a while. |
| Seat block | A set of seats allocated to one till in advance, so it can sell them offline. |
| Hold service | The component that grants holds, one concert at a time. |
| Web shop | The website where fans buy tickets online. |
